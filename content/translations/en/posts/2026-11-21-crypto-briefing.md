Bitcoin has lost the $60,000 level. On November 20 (local time), it fell as low as $57,778 during the session, its lowest since September 2024, and is now down more than 50% from its all-time high of around $126,000 set in October 2025. U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs saw record-sized outflows, while a sharp sell-off in artificial intelligence (AI) tech stocks and expectations of a December Federal Reserve rate hike dragged risk assets lower across the board. The Fear & Greed Index sits at 10–12, in "extreme fear" territory.

## The $60,000 Break: First Time Since July, Lowest Since September 2024

Bitcoin was trading near $61,400 at 9:40 a.m. ET on November 20 and slid to about $59,600 within roughly an hour, giving up the $60,000 level. It kept falling to an intraday low of $57,778 and closed at $58,317, its lowest level since September 2024. The [Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/livecoverage/stock-market-today-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-11-20-2026/card/bitcoin-drops-below-60-000-as-tech-sell-off-intensifies-yjVP6pBiSHdcFlQnQkOH) linked the drop to an intensifying tech-stock sell-off, and [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/bitcoin-falls-below-60k-for-first-time-since-july-amid-broader-market-selloff) reported it as the first move below $60,000 since July.

Bitcoin is down more than 20% in November. [TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/cryptobriefing:8f62f094e094b:0-bitcoin-falls-over-20-in-november-as-etf-outflows-mount-but-history-offers-some-hope/) noted the month is on track to be one of the weakest Novembers in recent years, and [Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/markets/bitcoin-price-slips-58300-nasdaq) wrote that if the trend continues, it could be the worst monthly performance since June 2022.

## ETF Outflows: IBIT's Largest Single-Day Outflow on Record

The most direct pressure came from U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs. According to Farside Investors, U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $582.3 million in net outflows on November 19, including $541.1 million from BlackRock's IBIT alone — the fund's largest one-day outflow on record. [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/bitcoin-falls-below-60k-for-first-time-since-july-amid-broader-market-selloff) reported cumulative net outflows in November of about $3.47 billion. That followed $306.5 million in outflows on November 18, so the pace of withdrawals accelerated.

Forced liquidations of leveraged positions amplified the decline. More than $700 million in crypto positions were liquidated over 24 hours, about 86% of them long (bullish) bets. Liquidations later grew to $1.53 billion over 24 hours, with $1.4 billion of that in long positions. Bitcoin alone accounted for around $540 million in liquidations, according to [Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/markets/bitcoin-price-slips-58300-nasdaq).

## AI Tech Sell-Off and Fed Rate-Hike Expectations Collided

The prevailing analysis is that this decline came more from the macro environment than from problems inside crypto. James Butterfill, head of research at CoinShares, told Cointelegraph that Bitcoin's weakness appears driven more by the sell-off in AI stocks than by crypto-specific issues, adding that Bitcoin has increasingly traded alongside other liquidity-sensitive growth assets.

The Nasdaq fell 2–5% during the session on November 20, and Nvidia dropped as much as 12.5% in a single day. Anthropic, which listed on Nasdaq the previous day after an $81.3 billion IPO — the largest on record — fell about 10% for a second straight day. As doubts grow over whether AI infrastructure spending will translate into near-term profits, investors appear to have sold existing tech holdings and crypto to fund IPO allocations. Related stocks fell in tandem: Strategy (MSTR) dropped about 8% and Coinbase (COIN) about 3%.

Rate expectations added to the pressure. According to CME Group's FedWatch tool, traders see roughly a 94% chance the Federal Reserve raises rates in December. With persistent inflation fueling hawkish expectations, sentiment across risk assets has weakened.

## Altcoins Fell Harder; Fear & Greed Index in "Extreme Fear"

Altcoins fell more than Bitcoin. Ethereum dropped below $1,700 for the first time since July 3, reaching an intraday low of $1,577. BNB fell more than 9%, while Solana, XRP, Dogecoin, and Cardano each dropped 5–7%.

The Fear & Greed Index reads 10–12, in "extreme fear" territory. A month ago it was 63 ("greed"); a week ago it was 20, and it has since fallen to 10. Total crypto market capitalization has shrunk about 27% over the past month.

What stands out is the character of the decline: a "liquidity squeeze" in which institutional outflows and leveraged liquidations are happening at the same time. It is not the result of a single negative catalyst, but of ETF selling, a tech-stock correction, and rate-hike expectations arriving together. Whether a rebound comes depends on whether spot ETF flows, led by IBIT, stabilize and whether the tech-stock sell-off pauses.

## Questions and Answers

### Why does Bitcoin breaking $60,000 matter?

$60,000 was a psychological support level and a price range held since July. Its break triggered automatic liquidations and pushed prices to their lowest since September 2024. The lows from July are now cited as the next support area to watch.

### How do ETF outflows affect the price?

Spot ETFs are a major channel for institutional money. Cumulative net outflows of $3.47 billion in November show selling pressure persisting while buying demand is weak. That said, not all long-term institutions have left: Goldman Sachs disclosed holdings of about $1.283 billion in spot Bitcoin ETFs as of the end of September.

### What signals could point to a rebound?

A Fear & Greed reading as low as 10 has sometimes marked short-term bottoms in past cycles, but it guarantees nothing on its own. A reversal in ETF flows and stabilization in tech stocks would need to come first.
