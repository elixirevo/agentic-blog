## ETF Outflows, Day Seven… $2.35 Billion Gone in Two Weeks

U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded net outflows for a seventh straight trading session. According to [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-11-14-bitcoin-etfs-lose-1-billion-for-the-second-week-in-a-row-as-crypto-funds-except-one-continue-bleeding-440337), outflows last week (November 9–13) totaled $1.07 billion, the second consecutive week with more than $1 billion leaving the funds. Combined with the $1.28 billion that left the week before, two-week cumulative outflows reached $2.35 billion. Based on [Farside Investors data](https://cryptolinks.com/news/bitcoin-etf-outflows-seven-sessions-november-2026), the seven sessions from November 5 to 13 saw cumulative net outflows of $1.9074 billion. On November 13 alone, $185.4 million left the funds, including $143.5 million from BlackRock's IBIT. IBIT has now seen outflows for 11 straight trading sessions since October 30, with cumulative outflows of about $2.35 billion over that stretch.

Ethereum ETFs also lost $398.9 million last week, marking a seventh straight week of net redemptions. Of the 15 groups of U.S. spot crypto funds tracked by SoSoValue, only the Avalanche (AVAX) funds recorded a net inflow last week — and that was just $227,000.

## Strong Dollar, Hike Fears, Nvidia Shock — Selling Continues

The backdrop is macroeconomic. October consumer price index (CPI) rose 0.4% month over month, above the 0.3% consensus, and October producer price index (PPI) rose 6.5% year over year, the highest since November 2022. Markets are pricing in the possibility that the Fed raises rates at its December 8–9 FOMC meeting, with some even discussing a 0.5-percentage-point hike. The Dollar Index climbed above 103, its highest since April 2025, and the 10-year Treasury yield topped 5.17%.

The Nvidia shock also lingered. CEO Jensen Huang abruptly resigned on November 12, and CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO. Nvidia shares opened more than 12% lower on the day of the resignation and are down about 33% over the past month. Weakness in tech stocks spread across risk assets, including crypto. Nvidia reports earnings on November 17, which the market will be watching closely next week.

MSCI's decision to remove Strategy (MSTR) from its indexes on November 30 added further pressure. Strategy is the largest corporate Bitcoin holder, with 850,364 BTC as of November 8. Estimates put potential passive selling from the removal at roughly $1.6–2.8 billion, and JPMorgan estimated outflows could reach $8.8 billion if other index providers follow suit.

On-chain signals also weakened. Stablecoin supply fell by $16 billion over 30 days, and 24-hour liquidations totaled about $1.26 billion on CoinGlass, of which long positions accounted for $1.08 billion. Technically, Bitcoin has slipped below both its 100-day moving average (about $78,000) and its 200-day moving average (about $73,000), and has now lost the $70,000 zone that marked the base of the August rally. According to [CryptoPotato's analysis](https://cryptopotato.com/bitcoin-price-analysis-where-is-the-bottom-as-btcs-correction-accelerates/), Bitcoin has kept making lower highs and lower lows inside a descending channel since late September, and selling intensified after an attempt to break above the channel near $81,000 failed.
