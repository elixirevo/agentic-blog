The Fear & Greed Index fell to 13, deepening the "extreme fear" zone. Buyers did step in at the lows, however, recovering part of the decline.

## About $2 Billion in Weekend Liquidations Hit Longs Hard

Leveraged liquidations accelerated the decline. CoinGlass data shows roughly $1.96–$2.02 billion in crypto positions were forcibly closed over 24 hours, with long positions accounting for about $1.68 billion of that total. Bitcoin liquidations ran about $750–$774 million, and Ethereum about $488 million. The [Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/markets/cryptocurrency/bitcoin-tumbles-to-four-month-low-as-liquidations-accelerate/articleshow/135846837.cms) noted that Bitcoin liquidation events above $500 million have become a recurring feature of the recent market.

The sell-off that began after Friday's U.S. market close continued all weekend. With U.S. equities closed, Bitcoin gave up the $70,000 level and slid to $62,000 before dip buyers emerged on Sunday morning. Total crypto market capitalization fell to about $2.28 trillion.

## Seven Straight Days of ETF Outflows… IBIT Accounts for 90%

Outflows from U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs show no sign of stopping. Farside Investors data shows seven consecutive trading days of net outflows from November 5 to 13, totaling about $1.91 billion. The week of November 9–13 alone saw $1.065 billion leave, including $185.4 million on November 13. BlackRock's IBIT accounted for $143.5 million of that day's outflows.

According to [CryptoNews](https://cryptonews.net/news/bitcoin/33754946/), IBIT has now posted 11 straight sessions of outflows since October 30, totaling about $2.35 billion — roughly 90% of all November outflows. Total net outflows across all U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs from October 27 to November 13 reached about $2.66 billion. Total ETF assets under management have slipped to about $94.9 billion, below the $100 billion mark they held through much of October. With ETFs closed over the weekend, whether outflows continue in Monday's session is the first test of the week.

## Rate-Hike Fears, the AI Bubble Debate, and Miner Selling Piled On

The backdrop is macroeconomic. October U.S. CPI rose 0.4% month over month, beating expectations, and Coinbase Institutional research dated November 13 said futures markets were pricing 64 basis points of Federal Reserve rate hikes over the following year, up sharply from 37 basis points in early November. The dollar index is above 103 and the 10-year Treasury yield above 5.17%, weighing on risk assets across the board.

Binance CEO Richard Teng said in an X post on November 15 that the sell-off was a "normal" reaction to tighter financial conditions, pointing to uncertainty around artificial intelligence financing. According to [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/binance-ceo-bitcoin-selloff-etf-outflows/), Binance Research found aggressive selling exceeded aggressive buying by 7,438 BTC on a dollar-based exchange between November 11 and 14, and described Bitcoin's 15.6% drop over 11 days as its steepest comparable sell-off since June.

Supply-side headwinds continued as well. Miner MARA Holdings disclosed the sale of 13,597 BTC (about $1.1 billion) after the third quarter, using $750 million of the proceeds to repay Bitcoin-backed loans. Coinbase Institutional estimated the combined circulating supply of major stablecoins (USDT, USDC, DAI) contracted by $11.5 billion over 30 days.

In South Korea, the kimchi premium stood out again. According to [CryptoCompass](https://cryptocompass.com/articles/bitcoin-records-2-85-kimchi-premium-in-south-korea-on-november-16-2026), Bitcoin traded at 89.26 million KRW on Upbit at midnight KST on November 16, a premium of about 2.85% over Binance (86.78 million KRW) — a sign that domestic buying was stronger than overseas.

## Technical Picture: The 200-Week Moving Average Is Now Resistance

Technically, Bitcoin has fallen below its 200-week moving average (about $67,750), which now acts as resistance. The 200-day moving average sits near $73,000. According to a [weekly levels analysis](https://tylerdurden.substack.com/p/bitcoin-levels-to-watch-nov-16-2026), the August low of $62,631 has already been breached; below lie the June low of $58,500 and the realized price near $53,900 as the next support candidates. The year's low is $57,742, reached on July 1. Where the weekly candle closes will decide whether this was a wick or a confirmed break below the 200-week average.

## Anticipated Questions

### Where is Bitcoin's next support?

The weekend low near $62,000 is the first support, followed by the $60,000 zone, the June low of $58,500, and the year's low of $57,742. Overhead, the 200-week moving average (about $67,750) is the first resistance. These are analysts' observations, not confirmed levels.

### What would stop the ETF outflows?

A cooling of macro uncertainty. If remarks from Chair Warsh, Nvidia's earnings, or the FOMC minutes ease rate-hike fears, inflows could resume. If hike fears grow, outflows are likely to continue.

### Why did prices fall so sharply over the weekend?

Selling that began after Friday's U.S. market close continued all weekend, and leveraged long liquidations (about $2 billion) accelerated the drop. With ETFs closed over the weekend, there were no institutional buyers to absorb the selling. This explains the direct cause of the decline, not the future direction.
