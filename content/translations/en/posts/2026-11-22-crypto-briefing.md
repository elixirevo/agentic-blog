Bitcoin fell to near $55,000 in the early hours of Saturday — its lowest in two years — before reclaiming $60,000 within hours. The crash was driven less by crypto-specific bad news than by a macro shock from the United States. Hotter-than-expected October inflation data raised the odds of a Federal Reserve rate hike in December, and a sharp sell-off in AI stocks dragged the Nasdaq down, triggering a broad risk-asset sell-off. The rebound came as bargain hunters stepped in amid extreme fear, but with spot ETF outflows continuing, it is still unclear whether the recovery will last.

## How the Weekend Crash Unfolded

Bitcoin traded above $70,000 early in the week, then lost $65,000 on Tuesday, $62,000 on Thursday, and $60,000 on Friday in succession. Selling accelerated as U.S. markets opened on Friday, pushing Bitcoin to $57,778 — below the July 1 low of $57,800. Early Saturday it fell to $55,130, its lowest since September 2024. [The Apex Wire](https://theapexwire.com/crypto/bitcoin-crashes-55k-rebounds-60k-liquidations/) reported that this low was the lowest since September 2024 according to CoinGecko.

Leveraged liquidations amplified the decline. According to [CoinGlass](https://tradersunion.com/news/cryptocurrency-news/show/4355827-bitcoin-plunges-to-55400/) data, total liquidations over 24 hours reached about $2.55 billion, of which long positions accounted for $2.27 billion. Bitcoin liquidations were the largest at about $1.16 billion, followed by Ethereum at $584 million and Solana at $67 million. Around 359,000 traders were forcibly liquidated.

The rebound was fast as well. Bitcoin rose about 9.8% from the $55,130 low to $60,522, and as of Saturday night it is trading above $60,000. The key question is whether it can hold $60,000 on a daily closing basis. One market report ([Ted Pillows](https://tedpillows.substack.com/p/crypto-and-finance-market-report-13a)) argued that a daily close above $60,000 would signal a return to the July range, while a settle below $55,130 would put the September 2024 low of $54,592 in view as the next support.

## Causes of the Crash: Rate-Hike Fears and the AI Stock Rout

The direct trigger was macroeconomic. Hotter-than-expected October inflation data pushed up the probability of a December Fed rate hike. According to CME FedWatch, the odds of a hike at the December 8–9 meeting rose to 93.5%, [The Apex Wire](https://theapexwire.com/crypto/bitcoin-crashes-55k-rebounds-60k-liquidations/) reported.

At the same time, doubts about the AI trade erupted. The Nasdaq Composite fell about 10% for the week, including a 5.3% drop on Friday alone. Nvidia plunged nearly 14% over the week. The S&P 500 also fell 3.7% on Friday, entering correction territory. As skepticism grew over whether massive AI infrastructure spending will ever generate returns, the tech sell-off spilled into digital assets. Andy Baehr of GSR told [The Business Times](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/companies-markets/banking-finance/bitcoin-falls-more-two-year-low-global-markets-sink) that "Bitcoin is along for the ride here, typically weak in deleveraging environments."

## ETF Outflows Continue

Institutional flows remain negative. According to [SoSoValue](https://cryptocompass.com/articles/crypto-etf-news-today-btc-eth-xrp-outflows-as-market-crash-hard) data, U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded net outflows of $582 million on November 19 and $124.43 million on November 20. The November 20 outflow was led by Fidelity's FBTC ($72.38 million) and Grayscale's GBTC ($32.84 million); BlackRock's IBIT figures had not yet been updated, so the final total could be larger. Ethereum ETFs also lost $13.18 million on November 20, and XRP ETFs shed $4.97 million. On a seven-day basis, Bitcoin ETF outflows total $1.64 billion. According to [The Business Times](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/companies-markets/banking-finance/bitcoin-falls-more-two-year-low-global-markets-sink), year-to-date Bitcoin ETF outflows have now exceeded $3 billion.

Bitcoin is down about 20% in November, giving back all of its gains since Donald Trump's re-election. A seventh consecutive weekly decline looks likely. [The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/markets/us-stocks/wall-street-guide/bitcoin-price-plunges-20-in-november-to-56000-as-institutional-outflows-and-us-ai-tech-stock-selloff-weigh-on-crypto/articleshow/136047312.cms) noted that despite November historically being a strong month — positive in 10 of the past 16 years — Bitcoin has fallen more than 20% this month.

## On-Chain and Market Signals Beneath the Surface

Several underlying indicators are also worth noting. JPMorgan estimated Bitcoin's production cost at about $86,000, well above the current spot price. As [CoinEdition](https://coinedition.com/24-hour-crypto-recap-heres-what-happened-in-the-market-104/) reported, that estimate suggests growing margin pressure on miners.

There was also movement on the stablecoin side. Sky's USDS circulating supply fell about 39% in 24 hours, from about 5.24 billion to 3.21 billion tokens, and Tether has burned 8 billion USDT on Ethereum over the past 20 days. Meanwhile, an address tied to the Binance hack moved about 197,000 ETH (roughly $346 million) to 17 addresses in about 16 minutes.

In addition, VanEck said it will end Gemini's custody and clearing role for its Bitcoin, Ether, and Solana ETFs, effective around February 16, 2027. [The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/coverage/stock-market-today/2026/11/20/crypto-market-today-nov-20-bitcoin-plunges-below-usd60-000-as-wall-street-s-sell-off-spreads-to-crypto/) pointed out that the simultaneous declines in stocks, gold, and cryptocurrencies suggest investors are prioritizing liquidity over risk-taking.

As [The Telegraph](https://thetelegraph.co.za/2026/11/21/bitcoin-rises-above-60000-after-crash-key-factors-that-may-support-continued-recovery/) reported, all three would need to be met for the rebound to turn into a trend reversal.
