## The Weekend Rebound: From a $55K Low Back to $60K

Bitcoin slid to $55,130 in the early hours of Saturday, its lowest since September 2024. Bargain hunting and short liquidations then drove a fast recovery, and by Sunday it was trading above $60,000. According to [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/bitcoin-price-pi-network-vvv-weekend-recovery/), Bitcoin reached a 24-hour high of $60,850 before pausing near $60,424 as upward momentum faded.

Ethereum also fell as low as $1,542.97 before recovering above $1,700, XRP bounced from a low of $0.8623 to the $0.92 range, and Solana climbed from $65.03 back to $70. [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/bitcoin-price-crash-seven-market-pressures-altcoins/) quoted investor Kyle Chassé's take on the sell-off: "Everyone wants one reason. There are seven, and they all piled on this week." The rebound was as fast as the drop, but on a weekly basis major assets are still deep in the red — Bitcoin -6.9%, Ethereum -6.6%.

Liquidations were large as well. Figures cited by [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/bitcoin-price-crash-seven-market-pressures-altcoins/) put 24-hour liquidations at roughly $2.7 billion during the sell-off. Forced liquidations deepened the decline, and short liquidations then helped fuel the bounce.

## Two Walls Blocking the Rebound: ETF Outflows and Rate-Hike Fears

Institutional flows remain negative despite the rebound. According to [Farside Investors](https://finance.biggo.com/news/15e86808-e2d5-486c-ba53-b3ce95ba7e16) data, U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs saw $1.1208 billion in net outflows from November 16–19, including $582.3 million on November 19 alone. BlackRock's IBIT accounted for $541.1 million of that day, its largest single-day outflow since launch. Cumulative November outflows have swelled to about $3.47 billion. Ethereum ETFs lost another $221.7 million over the same period.

Fed policy uncertainty also lingers. On November 18, Fed Governor Christopher Waller raised the possibility of a 0.5-percentage-point hike in December, and the FOMC minutes released the same day also leaned toward further increases. October core CPI rose 0.3% month on month, meeting the condition Waller had set, and markets are pricing a high probability of a hike at the December 8–9 meeting. Two-year U.S. Treasury yields hovering near 4.7% add further pressure on risk assets. However, UOB noted that as of November 18, futures pricing implied a quarter-point hike as the base case, with only about a 7.6% chance of an additional increase, [Newisty](https://newisty.com/blog/bitcoin-drops-below-60000-as-etf-outflows-grow-and-december-fed-decision-nears) reported — a sign the debate over the size of the move is not settled.

Tech-stock weakness added to the pressure. Despite positive catalysts — Nvidia's strong earnings on November 17 and Anthropic's Nasdaq listing on November 19 — the tech slump did not ease, and reports that the SEC is examining Nvidia's accounting practices piled on. The AI-bubble debate spilled over into a broad risk-asset sell-off.

## What to Watch This Week

The biggest variable is the October Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index, due Wednesday, November 25. Wall Street forecasts a 0.3% month-on-month rise, with core PCE also expected to climb 0.3%. Because it is the gauge the Fed uses as its policy benchmark, a hotter-than-expected reading could bring the possibility of a 0.5-percentage-point hike back into focus and prolong the crypto slump. The second estimate of third-quarter GDP is also due the same day, and Thursday is Thanksgiving, a U.S. market holiday. The Fed's rate decision is scheduled for December 9.

Singapore digital-asset trading firm QCP Capital named three preconditions for a meaningful price recovery: tech-stock stabilization, easing rate volatility, and renewed institutional inflows. Until all three are met, the battle around the $60,000 level is likely to continue. Conversely, if Bitcoin holds above $60,000 on daily closes and ETF outflows stop, the $55,130 low could harden into a short-term bottom.
