## Spot ETF Outflows Continue, but Cumulative Inflows Hold

Outflows from U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs continued. According to [FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/cryptocurrencies/news/crypto-today-bitcoin-ethereum-xrp-rise-etf-capital-outflows-202611191125), $306 million flowed out on November 18 alone, bringing the two-day total to $538 million. Spot Ethereum ETFs also recorded $40 million in net outflows on November 18, with net assets falling to $9.7 billion.

The longer-term picture has not broken down, however. Cumulative net inflows for spot Bitcoin ETFs remain at $54 billion, and for spot Ethereum ETFs at $11.4 billion. As long as short-term outflows stay well below those cumulative totals, it is hard to call this a wholesale institutional exit.

## Goldman Sachs Discloses Over $1.2 Billion in BTC ETF Holdings

Institutional news also cut the other way. In a Form 13F filing with the SEC, Goldman Sachs disclosed spot Bitcoin ETF holdings of about $1.283 billion as of September 30, 2026. The largest position was BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT), and its position in Fidelity's Wise Origin Bitcoin Fund (FBTC) grew more than 50-fold during the quarter. Over the same period, its Ethereum ETF holdings rose nearly sixfold to about $1.22 billion. Its long position in Strategy (MSTR), by contrast, was cut by about half to around $422.6 million. ([TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/cryptobriefing:15cf7d58e094b:0-goldman-sachs-discloses-over-1b-in-spot-bitcoin-etf-holdings/))

One caveat: a 13F filing is a snapshot at quarter-end. Bitcoin has fallen sharply since September 30, so the current value of these positions likely differs, and the filing alone does not reveal why Goldman holds them. Even so, the fact that a major bank maintains ETF exposure worth more than $1 billion shows institutional interest has not disappeared.

## BlackRock Signals Product Expansion — and Sees a Bigger Opportunity in Tokenization

Robbie Mitchnick, BlackRock's head of digital assets, said the firm plans to selectively expand its crypto product lineup as institutional demand grows. According to [KuCoin News](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/blackrock-plans-selective-crypto-product-expansion-amid-rising-institutional-demand), IBIT keeps drawing inflows despite weak sentiment, and the Bitcoin Premium Income ETF (BITA) has attracted $100 million. The Ethereum Staking ETF (ETHB), launched in March, has surpassed $1 billion in assets under management, and Mitchnick expects staking ETFs to account for most future Ethereum inflows. He also said tokenization is a bigger long-term opportunity than ETFs. He expects regulatory uncertainty and the stalled Clarity Act to have little effect on BlackRock's strategy, since Bitcoin and Ethereum already have regulatory clarity.

## Russia Opens Bitcoin and Ethereum to Retail Investors

The Bank of Russia has allowed ordinary retail investors to buy Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Tether (USDT) since November 17. According to [AltcoinBuzz](https://www.altcoinbuzz.io/crypto-news-today-russia-s-crypto-shift-institutional-etf-moves-and-trezor-data-breach), purchases are capped at 300,000 rubles per year through any single intermediary, and buyers must pass a test and read a risk warning before their first trade. The assets were chosen based on market capitalization, average daily trading volume, and at least five years of pricing history on foreign exchanges. From the same date, exchanges may admit the three assets to public trading. The immediate flow effect is likely small given the cap, but the direction matters: another major country is bringing retail crypto access inside the regulated system.

## Market Take: Fear Is Extreme, but Direction Is Not Yet Decided

The hawkish tone confirmed by the FOMC minutes and the prospect of a December rate hike still weigh heavily on the market. Two consecutive days of spot ETF outflows have kept short-term buying sentiment subdued, and a Fear & Greed Index reading of 10 shows investors are extremely cautious.

Yet the price is holding the $62,000 line, and major altcoins — Ethereum, BNB, Solana, and Cardano — were slightly higher or flat over 24 hours. Goldman Sachs' disclosure and BlackRock's product-expansion comments suggest long-term institutional interest is intact. In the short term, the key questions are whether the $62,000 support holds and whether ETF flows turn around. With neither direction confirmed, range-bound trading between support and resistance is the most likely near-term path.

## Questions and Answers

### Why does holding $62,000 matter for Bitcoin?

$62,000 is a support level that has been tested repeatedly over the past few days. If it breaks, the next support at the $61,400 level and the zone below it open up, and the decline could accelerate. Holding above it keeps the case for a short-term bottom alive.

### Why do cumulative inflows matter if spot ETF outflows continue?

A day or two of outflows is a short-term sentiment indicator, but $54 billion in cumulative net inflows shows how much institutional money has entered since the ETFs launched. Until short-term outflows approach a scale that reverses those cumulative inflows, this is hard to describe as a structural exit.

### What is the impact of Goldman Sachs' disclosure?

The 13F filing is a snapshot as of September 30 and does not reflect current values. Still, the fact that a major bank holds more than $1 billion in spot ETF exposure signals that institutional interest is holding up even during a price correction.

### Will Russia opening retail access move prices?

The annual cap of 300,000 rubles (a few thousand dollars) limits the immediate flow effect on global markets. The significance is directional: another major country is moving retail crypto access into the regulated system.
