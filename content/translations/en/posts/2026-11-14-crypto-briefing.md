## PPI Came In Below Forecast, but Fed Hike Fears Remain

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on November 13 that October PPI rose 0.3% month over month, slightly below the 0.4% consensus forecast. But the year-over-year rate hit 6.5%, the highest since November 2022, and core PPI, excluding food and energy, rose 5.0% year over year. According to [The Coin Republic](https://www.thecoinrepublic.com/2026/11/13/us-ppi-inflation-rises-65-as-markets-weigh-size-of-december-fed-rate-hike/), Bitcoin bounced 1.35% right after the release and briefly reclaimed $70,000, only to slide again soon after.

Markets are pricing in the possibility of a Fed rate hike ahead of the December 8–9 FOMC meeting. The dollar index climbed above 103, its highest since April 2025, and Bank of America expects consecutive hikes in December, January, and March. Although PPI came in below expectations, the annual figure itself was so high that it was not enough to dispel rate-hike concerns.

## Fifth Straight Day of ETF Outflows… IBIT Bleeds for a Ninth Day

US spot Bitcoin ETFs have now seen net outflows for five consecutive trading days. Citing [SoSoValue data](https://www.coingabbar.com/en/crypto-etf-news-today-btc-eth-outflows-nvidia-ceo-resign), $299.8 million flowed out on November 11 alone, with BlackRock's IBIT accounting for $269.7 million — its ninth straight day of outflows. Cumulative outflows over the five days reached about $1.44 billion. Another $47.61 million in net outflows was recorded on November 12, but Fidelity and BlackRock figures had not yet been reflected, so the final total could be larger. Ethereum ETFs also lost $144.81 million on November 11.

There are mixed signals on the long-term holding side, however. Barclays raised its IBIT stake by 48% in the third quarter, to 3.94 million shares. Short-term outflows and institutional long-term position-building are happening at the same time.

## Nvidia Shock and Strategy's MSCI Exclusion Add Selling Pressure

One direct trigger for the drop was Nvidia. News of Jensen Huang's sudden resignation sent Nvidia shares down more than 7% in after-hours trading and 4.34% in the regular session. Colette Kress has taken over as interim CEO. Weakness in tech stocks spilled into the semiconductor ETF (SOXX) and then across risk assets, including crypto.

Adding to the pressure, MSCI decided to remove Strategy (MSTR) from its global indexes in the December 1 rebalance. Strategy warned the exclusion could force about $2.8 billion in selling, and JPMorgan estimated outflows could reach $8.8 billion if other index providers follow.

On-chain signals also weakened. Short-term holders sent 48,500 BTC to exchanges in 24 hours, 47,300 of them at a loss. Stablecoin supply shrank by $14.2 billion in 30 days, the fastest contraction since June 2022, and the Coinbase premium has been negative for 70 straight days. Leveraged liquidations totaled about $1.23 billion according to CoinGlass, including $653.5 million in Bitcoin long liquidations.

## Questions and Answers

### Where is Bitcoin's next support?

Analysts had pointed to the 200-day moving average and the $70,000–$71,500 zone as the first support. With Bitcoin now below that range, the next support could be around $65,000 and the August lows, according to market watchers. These are analysts' views, not confirmed levels.

### What would it take for ETF outflows to stop?

A turn would likely require easing macro uncertainty. If the December FOMC outcome and subsequent inflation data reduce rate-hike fears, inflows could resume. If inflation proves sticky, outflows are likely to continue.

### Is this a correction after the October peak, or a trend reversal?

Bitcoin is about 46% below its October 2025 record high of roughly $126,198. A five-session drop of more than 7%, combined with simultaneous ETF outflows, liquidations, and shrinking stablecoin supply, shows selling pressure beyond a brief pullback. But there are also long-term buying signals, such as Barclays increasing its IBIT stake, so it is too early to call a trend reversal.
