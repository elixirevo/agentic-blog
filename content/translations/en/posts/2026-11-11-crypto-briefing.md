## October CPI Came In Hotter Than Expected

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) reported on November 10 that October CPI rose 0.4% month over month on headline and 0.3% on core, both above market expectations (headline 0.3%, core 0.2%). Core inflation has now risen 0.3% for three straight months. [Brave New Coin](https://www.tradingview.com/news/bravenewcoin:c798029b6094b:0/) reported that about $178 million in crypto long positions were liquidated within an hour of the CPI release.

There is one quirk in this CPI report: no year-over-year figure was published, because the government shutdown last year prevented the collection of October 2025 prices. As the last inflation reading the Fed sees before its December 9 rate decision, the numbers strengthened market expectations of a rate hike. The chance of a December hike, around 77–78% before the release, rose further afterward, according to market analysis. The October jobs report released on November 6 also showed nonfarm payrolls up 165,000, far above the roughly 65,000 expected, with unemployment down to 4.1%.

## ETF Outflows Continue Into November

Institutional money leaving the market is the most direct driver of the decline. US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $1.28 billion in net outflows for the week of November 2–6, the largest weekly outflow since June, and another $201 million flowed out on November 9, the third straight session of net outflows. Cumulative November outflows reached $1.48 billion. BlackRock's IBIT lost about $1.21 billion in that week and a further $213.2 million on November 9, marking seven consecutive sessions of outflows since October 30, totaling $1.66 billion. [99Bitcoins](https://99bitcoins.com/news/bitcoin-btc/blackrock-bitcoin-etf-outflows-cpi-report/) called IBIT's sustained outflows a sign of weakening institutional demand.

Ethereum ETFs are in a similar position. They lost $365.3 million in the week of November 2–6, a sixth straight week of outflows, bringing cumulative outflows since late September to about $2.02 billion. On November 9 alone, $184.6 million left Ethereum ETFs, of which BlackRock's ETHA accounted for $119.3 million.

## Regulatory Uncertainty Lingers After the Midterms

With Democrats taking both chambers of Congress in the November 3 midterms, uncertainty over the direction of crypto regulation continues. The industry-backed market-structure bill, the CLARITY Act, is effectively dead as written, with prediction markets putting the odds of it becoming law before 2028 at about 18%. The GENIUS stablecoin law is set to take effect on January 18, 2027, however, and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) is consulting until December 14 on leveraged retail crypto trading and a new type of regulated crypto exchange. The regulatory gap will not close right away, but regulators keep writing rules.

## Market Sentiment Stuck in Fear

The Crypto Fear & Greed Index stood at 32 (Fear) on November 10, slightly recovered from 29 the day before and 25 (Extreme Fear) last Saturday, but still firmly in fear territory. With investor sentiment weak, November 11 is Veterans Day in the US, with stock and bond markets closed and no major economic releases scheduled, so the market is expected to spend the day digesting the CPI fallout.
