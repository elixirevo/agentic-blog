## ETF Outflows Led the Sell-Off

The most direct driver of the decline was institutional money leaving the market. US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $1.28 billion in net outflows for the week of November 2–6, the largest weekly outflow since June. BlackRock's IBIT alone accounted for about $1.21 billion of that total. On Friday alone, Bitcoin ETFs lost $412 million and Ethereum ETFs lost $93 million. According to [KuCoin News](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/bitcoin-falls-below-75-000-amid-etf-outflows-and-1b-in-liquidations), weekly outflows from Ethereum ETFs reached $365 million, the largest in four months.

[Analytics Insight](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/amp/story/cryptocurrency-analytics-insight/crypto-prices-today-bitcoin-slips-to-usd-74600-as-ether-and-xrp-lead-declines) reported that this week's outflows pushed year-to-date net inflows into Bitcoin ETFs into negative territory. With institutional demand weakening, a price rebound will be hard to sustain unless the outflows stop.

## Over $1 Billion in Liquidations; Technical Position Looks Fragile

As the price fell below $75,000, leveraged long positions were forcibly closed in a cascade. According to Coinglass data, total liquidations over 24 hours reached about $1.18 billion, of which long positions accounted for roughly $933 million, with more than 167,000 traders liquidated. [TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/dpa_afx:9da3601265f15:0-cryptos-extend-losses/) reported that total crypto market capitalization shrank to $2.50 trillion.

Technically, Bitcoin is barely holding after testing the $73,500–$74,000 zone. If that range breaks, the next support is cited near $72,150, while $80,000 stands as strong resistance above. Ethereum broke below its $2,200–$2,230 support and is trading around $2,130. On a weekly basis, Bitcoin is down about 4.6% and Ethereum 7.4%.

## Tonight's CPI Will Test December Rate-Hike Odds

The next turning point is the US October CPI, due at 8:30 AM ET (10:30 PM KST). The market consensus is for a 0.3% month-over-month rise in headline inflation and 0.2% in core. The Cleveland Fed model projects headline at 0.24% and core at 0.21%. [CoinStats](https://coinstats.app/ai/a/latest-news-for-bitcoin) noted that October Producer Price Index (PPI) data follows on November 13.

After a stronger-than-expected US jobs report, market-implied odds of a December Fed rate hike have risen to around 80%. A hotter-than-expected CPI reading could lift hike expectations further and weigh on risk assets, while a cooler print could ease hike fears and set up a rebound.

## Regulatory and Security Risks Also Weigh on Sentiment

Democratic victories in the US midterm elections have raised concerns about stricter cryptocurrency regulation. [Crypto.news](https://crypto.news/why-is-the-crypto-market-down-today-bitcoin-ethereum-in-red/) analyzed that the results have added uncertainty to the future of industry-backed legislation. Not all the news is negative, however. Charles Schwab added Solana (SOL), Avalanche (AVAX), and Chainlink (LINK) to its crypto trading platform, and Bitmine bought an additional 9,587 ETH. The broader institutional adoption trend has not broken.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Could Bitcoin fall further?

If the $73,500 support breaks, the next support is cited near $72,150. Some analysts see room down to $68,000 if support fails, but that is a forecast, not an established fact. Tonight's CPI result is likely to decide the near-term direction.

### What happens if ETF outflows continue?

ETFs are the main channel for institutional money. Continued outflows sustain spot selling pressure and weaken any rebound. Conversely, a return to net inflows could turn sentiment around quickly.

### When is the CPI release, and why does it matter?

It is released at 10:30 PM KST on November 10. The inflation reading changes expectations for the Fed's December rate decision. A lower-than-expected print would ease rate-hike fears, which is positive for crypto; a higher print would do the opposite.
