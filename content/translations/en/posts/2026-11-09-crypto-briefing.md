## ETF Outflows Hit a Four-Month High

Institutional outflows are the most direct driver of this decline. During the week of November 2–6, U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs saw net outflows of about $1.28 billion and Ethereum ETFs about $365 million — the largest weekly Bitcoin ETF outflow in four months. According to a [Medium roundup](https://medium.com/@ztxexexchange/ztxex-daily-crypto-news-highlights-5f85ea438c98), roughly $505 million left the two ETF groups combined on Friday alone.

Thursday was the heaviest day. Bitcoin ETFs lost $430 million, including $403 million from BlackRock's IBIT alone. Ethereum ETFs also recorded $100 million in net outflows that day. On Friday, however, outflows slowed to $51.7 million for Bitcoin ETFs and $1.9 million for Ethereum ETFs, suggesting selling pressure may be easing somewhat.

Liquidations continued in the derivatives market as well. Based on [Coinglass data](https://www.tradingview.com/news/dpa_afx:12c7f569f9281:0-cryptos-tumble-as-markets-focus-on-u-s-regulatory-outlook/), 24-hour liquidations surged to $1.22 billion on November 6 before falling back to around $500 million.

## Regulatory Uncertainty Weighs on the Market

After the AP called both chambers of Congress for the Democrats, concerns spread that the U.S. Congress could shift toward a less crypto-friendly regulatory stance. A [TradingView/dpa report](https://www.tradingview.com/news/dpa_afx:12c7f569f9281:0-cryptos-tumble-as-markets-focus-on-u-s-regulatory-outlook/) cited this as a key factor behind the past 24 hours of losses. On top of that, U.S. nonfarm payrolls rose by 165,000 in October — far above the 65,000 expected — pushing the dollar higher, while a selloff in AI and tech stocks dragged down risk assets broadly.

Regulatory news kept coming. The U.S. Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) began proceedings to cut exchange MEXC off from the U.S. banking system over alleged illicit transactions worth about $16 billion. OKX's CEO warned users not to transfer funds directly from MEXC to OKX. On the other hand, FinCEN and the Federal Reserve withdrew a proposed crypto transfer-tracking rule effective November 9, a reminder that regulation is not moving in only one direction.

Elsewhere, Russia will allow retail investors to buy BTC, ETH, and USDT through licensed intermediaries starting November 17, though non-qualified investors face an annual purchase cap of 300,000 rubles. Nigeria's central bank introduced a licensing framework for stablecoin issuers, and Ukraine's central bank formally opposed a draft virtual-assets bill.

## Bitcoin Falls Below Strategy's Average Purchase Price

Bitcoin dropped below Strategy's (formerly MicroStrategy) average purchase price of $75,451, putting its holdings at an unrealized loss of about $500 million. Strategy held 849,327 BTC as of November 1. The news itself did not move the market, but a flagship institutional holder slipping into the red is a symbolic marker of how deep the current decline runs.

## Anticipated Questions

### Could Bitcoin fall further?
Possibly. After the $74,000 level gave way, the next psychological support often cited is around $70,000, but that is a market view, not a confirmed fact. Monday's ETF flows and U.S. equity moves are likely to set the short-term direction.

### Is the slowdown in ETF outflows a rebound signal?
Friday's outflows were much smaller than Thursday's, which is encouraging, but a single day of data cannot confirm a trend reversal. Watch whether outflows slow further or turn into net inflows early next week.

### Is this decline a continuation of the late-October selloff?
It can be seen as part of the same flow. Bitcoin has stayed in a downtrend since late October and is down about 14% year to date, among the weakest major assets this year. The direct triggers, however, have shifted to post-midterm regulatory worries and ETF outflows.
