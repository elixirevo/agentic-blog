## Today's Key Stories

### 1. Spot ETF Outflows: $1.28 Billion in November, Year-to-Date Flows Turn Negative

US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded net outflows of $430.2 million on November 5 and $412 million on November 6, taking November outflows to about $1.28 billion. BlackRock's IBIT and Fidelity's FBTC led the selling on both days. November 4 was the only day this month with net inflows. ([SMW Markets](https://www.smwmarkets.com/bitcoin-75k-etf-outflows-november/), [CoinCentral](https://coincentral.com/bitcoin-btc-price-weekly-close-could-make-or-break-bitcoins-next-move/))

Spot Ethereum ETFs also lost $93.19 million on November 6, and on November 5, Bitcoin and Ethereum funds together shed $530 million. According to one tally, year-to-date net inflows into US spot Bitcoin ETFs have turned negative at minus $502 million — meaning all the institutional money that came in during 2026 has now flowed back out. ([CoinCentral](https://coincentral.com/bitcoin-btc-price-weekly-close-could-make-or-break-bitcoins-next-move/), [Interactive Crypto](https://www.interactivecrypto.com/bitcoin-s-2026-gains-erased-etf-outflows-overwhelm-regulatory-win))

ETF flows are a key signal for the short-term price direction. While outflows persist, rebound attempts are likely to run into selling. That said, outflows alone do not confirm a bear market, and a few more days of flow data are needed before judging the trend.

### 2. Aftershocks of the Jobs Surprise: December Rate-Hike Odds Near 80%

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on November 6 that October nonfarm payrolls rose by 165,000, far above the roughly 65,000 expected, while the unemployment rate eased from 4.2% to 4.1%. After the release, market-implied odds of a 25bp rate hike at the December FOMC rose from about 67% to 77–80%, and the US 10-year Treasury yield climbed to roughly 4.88%. ([CoinCentral](https://coincentral.com/bitcoin-btc-price-weekly-close-could-make-or-break-bitcoins-next-move/), [CryptosBank](https://cryptosbank.in/2026/11/07/bitcoin-holds-near-76000-despite-1-28-billion-in-spot-etf-withdrawals-and-broader-market-volatility/))

The Fed raised rates by 25bp in September and has kept the door open to further hikes, citing sticky inflation. The next FOMC meeting is December 8–9. Higher rates for longer reduce the relative appeal of risk assets and strengthen the dollar, which is a short-term headwind for crypto. Still, a jobs report is only a snapshot of labor conditions — it does not decide the Fed's next move by itself. The next jobs report comes out on December 4.

### 3. Regulatory Tailwind: FinCEN and the Fed Withdraw Crypto Transfer Tracking Proposal

On November 6, the US Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) and the Federal Reserve Board formally withdrew a controversial proposal that would have lowered the threshold for tracking international crypto transfers from $3,000 to $250. The withdrawal takes effect on November 9 and is widely seen as a win for financial privacy. ([Interactive Crypto](https://www.interactivecrypto.com/bitcoin-s-2026-gains-erased-etf-outflows-overwhelm-regulatory-win))

The move could ease compliance burdens for crypto institutions and support long-term adoption. But the market has not priced it in yet — macro headwinds and ETF flows are dominating in the near term. The broader regulatory picture remains uncertain. After Democrats retook the House in the midterms, reports emerged that a new market-structure bill expanding SEC oversight and shrinking the CFTC's role is under discussion, putting the market in a regulatory reassessment phase.

## Anticipated Questions and Answers

### Why is Bitcoin holding around $75,000?

The $75,000 area is where the November 3 low and Friday's low ($74,762) overlap, so it is acting as technical support. The 200-day moving average ($73,197) also sits below as support. But holding support does not guarantee the decline is over.

### What does it mean that all of this year's ETF inflows have flowed out?

One tally puts year-to-date net inflows into US spot Bitcoin ETFs at negative $502 million — more money has left the funds than came in during 2026. That is a cumulative flow figure, not a statement that the ETFs' Bitcoin holdings have vanished. This article is not investment advice.

### Why is FinCEN's withdrawal of the tracking proposal good news?

Lowering the tracking threshold from $3,000 to $250 would have brought even small transfers under surveillance, sharply raising compliance costs. Withdrawing it removes that burden and is viewed positively for financial privacy. It has not yet shown up in short-term prices, though.
